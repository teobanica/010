Spectral measure blowup for Hadamard subfactors
Banica, Codex and Fable

Code for the paper (010.tex, 010.pdf). Every program is mentioned in
the paper where it is used. Section 7 contains circulant families, moments, growth, Hankel/Jacobi
comparisons, and finite-depth laws with long generic truncations. All new checks are in coppi.py.
Run from this folder, with Python 3:

  python3 riis.py
  python3 zabel.py
  python3 armstrong.py
  python3 wordgram.py
  python3 pantani.py
  python3 jalabert.py
  python3 zulle.py
  python3 klein_moments.py
  python3 coppi.py

Each run takes from 1 second to 2 minutes. The programs riis, zabel, pantani, jalabert and zulle print
their results in the terminal and also write them to riis.log, zabel.log, pantani.log, jalabert.log
and zulle.log, which are included. The other programs print in the terminal only;
the captured output of coppi.py is included as coppi.log.

SECTION 1

riis.py           Standard library only. The 4 pairs of principal graphs of index between 4 and 5
                  (Haagerup, Asaeda-Haagerup, 3311, 2221): walk counts on both graphs of each pair,
                  annular multiplicities, the rooted laws of Theorem 1.8 in exact arithmetic in
                  Q(sqrt d), the closed formulas for the moments, the atoms of the lift.

SECTION 2

zabel.py          Standard library only. The 8 Lie algebras of the exceptional series: positive
                  roots from the Cartan matrices, the levels of Lemma 2.4, the identity of
                  Proposition 2.5, the signed measures and the dimensions of the Cartan powers of
                  the adjoint representation for k <= 6, all in exact rational arithmetic. Also
                  the circular measures in Section 1.2, Theorem 2.2 and Proposition 2.1, against
                  walk counts.

SECTIONS 3 AND 4

armstrong.py      Needs NumPy. Constructs deformed Fourier matrices and checks invariant tensor
                  dimensions with the finite tensor moment matrices of Remark 3.3. The eigenvalue
                  comparisons are numerical, with tolerance 1e-6.
wordgram.py       Needs SymPy. Compares exact return-word counts with Gram traces for the finite
                  phase matrices of Remark 4.5 and their transposes. It also checks the 3 atomic
                  laws at index 6, 7 moments at index 9, and the first exceptional moment of
                  Proposition 6.3 for m = 1, 2, 3, 4.
pantani.py        Standard library only. The generators g_{k,e} of Proposition 4.2 as exact Laurent
                  monomials in the free entries of Q, for all pairs of abelian groups of order at
                  most 6: the formula with the multiplicative double difference used in the proof
                  of Theorem 4.6, its symmetries, the transposition of the generators, and the
                  fact that the phase group is the whole torus for generic Q. Also the description of
                  the generators as dephased second differences of Q, used in Theorem A.

SECTION 5

jalabert.py       Standard library for the main part. Generic index 6 and 8: word counts in the
                  crossed coproduct group, closed walks on the honeycomb, kagome and diamond
                  lattices, and the formulas of Propositions 5.4 and 5.5. Theorems 5.10 and 5.11:
                  the parameters of every ideal of norm <= 60 of the Eisenstein integers, the
                  ideals coming from all roots of unity of order <= 36, and the separation of the
                  243 ideals of norm <= 400 by their rooted laws. With networkx: identification
                  of the first coset graphs (K_33, cube, Heawood, Pappus, Nauru). With SciPy: the
                  2 forms of the density of Theorem 5.15, against the moments. Also the relations
                  of the group p3, and the moments and annular multiplicities of the 3 finite
                  limits of the Karlsson matrices discussed at the end of Section 5.3.

SECTION 6

zulle.py          Needs NumPy for the grid integrations. Computes d, kappa, Delta_Q and C of
                  Theorem 6.1 from the exponents of the entries of Q, in exact arithmetic, and
                  compares C (MN)^n n^(-d/2) with the moments, for the 12 matrices listed in the
                  table of Section 6.1. Also, for Section 7: the circulant family on Z_3, Z_4,
                  Z_2^2, Z_5, Z_6, where d = N-1, kappa = 1, Delta = N^(N-4) and C is the
                  constant of Corollary 7.3, and the order l^d of the phase group of Theorem 7.6
                  in 7 cases, together with the group generated by q^4 at M = N = 2.
klein_moments.py  Standard library only. Exact moments for the Klein four family of Example 6.5,
                  with finite cyclic and circle phases.

SECTION 7

coppi.py         Standard library only, exact integer and rational arithmetic. Theorem 7.1:
                  phase-generator identities and ranks for cyclic groups of order 2 through 8,
                  and for Z_2^2, Z_4 x Z_2, Z_2^3 and Z_3^2. Proposition 7.2: independent
                  return-word counts and squared multinomial moments through degree 6 for
                  N = 2, 3, degree 5 for both groups of order 4, and degree 3 for N = 5;
                  generic third moments for N = 2 through 5. Corollary 7.3: the determinant
                  of the Laplace quadratic form for N = 2 through 10. Propositions 7.4-7.5:
                  the first odd Jacobi defect of the circulant family, and the delayed Hankel
                  and even Jacobi defects at index 8, for m = 1 through 5. In particular it
                  checks D_3 = 39 versus 47 and lambda_2 = 39/16 versus 47/16 at m = 1.
                  Theorem 7.6 and Corollary 7.7: the exponent matrix for constant free entries,
                  its determinant M^(N-1) N^(M-1), and the first extra moment at degree 2l.
                  Exact Hankel/Jacobi checks cover M = 2, N = 2 through 5, and orders l = 2
                  through 7 coprime to 2N. Direct Gram expansions cover (M,N,l) = (3,3,2)
                  and (3,5,2). Independent block-action return words check these laws as well
                  as index 6 with l = 5, index 8 with l = 3, and a noncyclic order-4 group.
                  The index-9 example has moments 1,1,5,29,185, D_3 = 64 and lambda_2 = 4,
                  versus 1,1,5,29,181, D_3 = 48 and lambda_2 = 3 in the generic case.

The source and the supplied log can be reproduced with:

  python3 coppi.py > coppi.log

The finite checks supplement the proofs in the paper. No numerical phase integration is used
in coppi.py, and no files outside this folder are needed to run it.
