# 010

## Spectral measure blowup for Hadamard subfactors

**Banica, Codex and Fable** (November 2026)

This repository contains the paper (`010.pdf`, `010.tex`) and the programs which check its computations (`code010`).

## Summary

For a subfactor, the moments of the spectral measure of the principal graph at its root are the dimensions of the box spaces. A blowup of this measure is a realization of the same numbers on a space where the measure becomes simpler. The paper studies this question for the Hadamard subfactors coming from the deformed Fourier matrices H = F_X ⊗_Q F_Y, where X, Y are finite abelian groups of orders M, N, and Q is a matrix of parameters on the unit circle.

Main results:

- **Gram blowup, for all parameters.** There is a compact abelian group K_Q of M × N phase matrices, generated by the dephased second differences of Q, such that the law is (1 − 1/N) δ_0 + (1/N) law(ZZ*), with Z Haar distributed on K_Q. This group is the full torus of dimension (M − 1)(N − 1) for generic Q, and is finite when the entries of Q are roots of unity.
- **Coverings.** K_Q is the group of flat connections on an abelian covering of the complete bipartite graph K_{X,Y}, which is the maximal one in the generic case.
- **Rigidity.** The generic moments are lower bounds for all the moments, with equality precisely when the quantum group is the generic one, and the first strict inequality is at the length of the shortest relation. On the other hand, H and its transpose have the same law, while their quantum groups are different for M ≠ N.
- **Growth.** The moments grow as C (MN)^n n^(−d/2), with d = dim K_Q and an explicit constant C.
- **Index 6.** The relation subgroups are exactly the ideals of the Eisenstein integers. The generic law comes from the honeycomb and kagome lattices, with density given by the 3-step planar random walk, and the finite cases give the cube, Heawood, Pappus and Nauru graphs.
- **Indices 8 and 9, and square indices.** The diamond lattice and arbitrarily delayed exceptional moments at index 8, the triangular lattice at index 9, and circulant families at index N², whose laws are those of N-step planar random walks.
- **Jacobi parameters.** The first Jacobi defect is located, and there are finite depth laws whose moments agree with the generic ones up to any prescribed order.

Sections 1 and 2 contain preliminaries: the rooted laws of the Haagerup, Asaeda-Haagerup, 3311 and 2221 subfactors, and signed root measures along the exceptional series of Deligne.

## Programs

Every program is mentioned in the paper at the place where it is used.

| Program | Paper | Content | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `riis.py` | Section 1 | the 4 pairs of principal graphs of index between 4 and 5: walk counts, annular multiplicities, the rooted laws of Theorem 1.8 in exact arithmetic | standard library |
| `zabel.py` | Sections 1.2 and 2 | circular measures against walk counts; the 8 Lie algebras of the exceptional series: roots, levels, signed measures, dimensions of the Cartan powers (Lemma 2.4, Proposition 2.5, Theorem 2.6) | standard library |
| `armstrong.py` | Section 3 | deformed Fourier matrices and invariant tensor dimensions, as in Remark 3.3 (numerical, tolerance 1e-6) | NumPy |
| `wordgram.py` | Sections 4, 5, 6 | return-word counts against Gram traces (Remark 4.5), 3 atomic laws at index 6, 7 moments at index 9, Proposition 6.3 | SymPy |
| `pantani.py` | Section 4 | the generators of Proposition 4.2 as exact Laurent monomials, for all pairs of abelian groups of order at most 6; transposition (Theorem 4.6); second differences (Theorem A) | standard library |
| `jalabert.py` | Section 5 | word counts and closed walks on the honeycomb, kagome and diamond lattices; Eisenstein ideals (Theorems 5.10 and 5.11); the first coset graphs; the density of Theorem 5.15 | standard library, networkx and SciPy for 2 parts |
| `zulle.py` | Sections 6 and 7 | the constants of Theorem 6.1 from the exponents of Q, against the moments, for 12 matrices; the circulant family (Corollary 7.3) and the finite phase groups of Theorem 7.6 | NumPy |
| `klein_moments.py` | Section 6 | exact moments for the Klein four family of Example 6.5 | standard library |
| `coppi.py` | Section 7 | circulant families, moments, Hankel and Jacobi defects, finite depth laws (Theorems 7.1 and 7.6, Propositions 7.2 to 7.5, Corollaries 7.3 and 7.7), in exact arithmetic | standard library |

## Running the code

With Python 3, from the folder `code010`:

    python3 riis.py
    python3 zabel.py
    python3 armstrong.py
    python3 wordgram.py
    python3 pantani.py
    python3 jalabert.py
    python3 zulle.py
    python3 klein_moments.py
    python3 coppi.py

Each run takes from 1 second to 2 minutes. The programs `riis`, `zabel`, `pantani`, `jalabert` and `zulle` print their results in the terminal and also write them to their `.log` files, which are included. The output of `coppi.py` is included as `coppi.log`, and can be reproduced with `python3 coppi.py > coppi.log`. More details are in `code010/README.txt`.
